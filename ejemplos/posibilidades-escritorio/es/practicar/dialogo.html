At the school library

Material original y ficticio para la formación. Las situaciones, el centro y los datos son inventados; no contiene información de alumnado real. No es una transcripción del currículo oficial.

Contexto de lectura

Alex y Sam preparan un cartel sobre animales de río. Visitan una biblioteca escolar ficticia para elegir fuentes y una lectura personal. La conversación contiene peticiones, normas de préstamo, referencias a lugares y decisiones que deben justificarse con lo que dice cada personaje.

Es un texto original en inglés. Puede utilizarse como lectura dramatizada o como base para comprensión escrita en los primeros cursos de ESO, ajustando la ayuda lingüística al grupo. No se atribuye a un examen oficial ni presupone un nivel certificado.

At the school library

Librarian: Good morning. Welcome to the school library. Are you looking for a book for yourself or for a class project?

Alex: Both, actually. We are doing a project about animals, but I would also like something to read at home.

Sam: I am helping Alex with the project. We need information about animals that live near rivers. We do not need a very long book.

Librarian: The information books are on the blue shelf, next to the window. The stories and comics are on the green shelf.

Alex: Is this book about river animals a good choice? It has photographs and a page about otters.

Librarian: Yes. It also has a short glossary at the back. You can use that if you find a new word.

Sam: Can we borrow it until next Monday? Our class is making a poster at the end of the week.

Librarian: Normally you can borrow a book for one week. Today is Monday, so next Monday is fine. If you need more time, ask before the return date.

Alex: Can I take two books? I would like this comic as well. It is about two children who build a boat.

Librarian: Yes. Each student can borrow two books at a time. Please bring your library card so I can record them.

Sam: I left my card in the classroom. Can I borrow a book using Alex's card?

Librarian: No, please use your own card. You can look at the books here while Alex chooses, then come back with your card after class.

Sam: That is all right. I will write down the title of the book I want. Where can we sit and look at the river book?

Librarian: There is a reading table by the door. Please speak quietly because another group is reading there.

Alex: This page says that otters need clean water. Can we copy the whole page for our poster?

Librarian: For this activity, read the information, choose two useful facts and explain them in your own words. Write down the title and the page you used. Ask your teacher if you are unsure.

Sam: We could compare the photographs first. Then we can choose one fact each and check that the book really says it.

Alex: Good idea. I will borrow the river book and the comic. We can use the river book in class tomorrow.

Librarian: Here you are. Both books are due next Monday. Keep them in a dry place and tell me if you notice a damaged page.

Sam: Thank you. I will bring my own card later. I would like to read the story about the old bridge.

Librarian: You can find that story on the green shelf. There is no need to hurry: the library is open again after lunch.

Alex: Thanks for your help. Let us sit at the reading table and choose the facts for our poster.

Objetivos para preparar preguntas y criterios

  1. Localizar información explícita: los libros elegidos, los lugares y la fecha de devolución.
  2. Distinguir quién formula una petición y quién explica una norma.
  3. Justificar las respuestas con una frase o un turno concreto del diálogo.
  4. Reconocer una decisión de los personajes sin inventar lo que ocurrirá después.

La práctica tendrá cuatro preguntas en inglés y un solucionario docente separado. Conviene combinar una pregunta de localización, una sobre una norma, una sobre la razón de una decisión y otra que pida apoyar la respuesta con el texto. Si una pregunta admite varias respuestas razonables, el solucionario debe reconocerlo.

Criterios observables para una rúbrica breve

  • Comprensión: la respuesta se ajusta al diálogo y no añade datos que no aparecen.
  • Evidencia: identifica una frase o intervención que permite sostenerla.
  • Comunicación: expresa la idea de manera comprensible con la ayuda lingüística acordada.

No se debería confundir automáticamente un error gramatical con una falta de comprensión. El profesorado decide cuánto pesa cada aspecto según el propósito de la actividad. Esta ficha no incluye respuestas para conservarlas en el documento docente que se prepare después.
